# StarCell — Player Arc

StarCell has no scripted story and no authored progression path. The player arc described here is a typical emergent experience — what a player is likely to encounter if they engage with the world's systems in good faith. It is a design target for pacing and system readiness, not a campaign outline.

The arc should feel like an open-world RPG where the world does things to the player as much as the player does things to the world. The key emotional beats are: discovery → investment → consequence → legacy.

---

## Early Game (Hour 1)

The player spawns into an unfamiliar zone with minimal gear and no established relationships. The first hour is about reading the world and finding a foothold.

**Typical experience:**
- Discover cell types and what can be harvested; learn basic movement and interaction
- Encounter first NPCs — some passive, some hostile; learn the threat gradient
- Find or build first shelter; survive first night cycle
- Discover the inventory, crafting, and spell systems through natural exploration
- Complete a first quest or establish a first NPC relationship

**Design targets:**
- No tutorial; the world should be readable enough to learn from observation
- Player should encounter at least one moment of genuine surprise in the first hour
- Death should feel recoverable — a setback, not a reset
- The world should feel inhabited from the first minute, not empty until the player builds something

---

## Mid Game

The player has a foothold — some gear, some NPCs, some understanding of the world. Mid game is about expanding influence and deepening investment.

**Typical experience:**
- Build or claim a structure; begin attracting or protecting NPCs
- Travel to adjacent zones; discover new biomes, factions, and threats
- Develop a companion or follower relationship — someone whose fate matters
- Begin accumulating gold and better gear through quests and exploration
- First major consequence event: a raid, an NPC death, a faction power shift

**Design targets:**
- Mid game should feel like the world is reacting to the player — not just the player reacting to the world
- NPCs should start to feel distinct. One blacksmith should not be identical to another.
- The player should have something they care about losing — a companion, a structure, a hard-earned item
- Zone travel should reveal that the world is much larger than the starting area

---

## Late Game

The player has significant power and established presence in the world. Late game is about legacy, consequence at scale, and the world's deep systems surfacing.

**Typical experience:**
- Zone influence spans multiple areas; player's faction standing matters
- Named villains, legendary items, and ancient ruins become accessible
- NPC society has matured — villages have histories, monuments, generational characters
- Major consequence events escalate: faction wars, ecological collapse, named villain emergence
- Player makes choices about what to protect, what to sacrifice, and what to leave behind

**Design targets:**
- Late game should not feel like a power fantasy alone — the stakes should still be real
- NPC aging and death should be felt. Long-term companions dying should be meaningful, not just a stat loss.
- The world should look visibly different from hour one — changed by the player and by time

---

## Milestone Moments

These are the high-value emotional beats the systems should reliably produce. They are not scripted — they emerge from system interactions.

- **First follower** — an NPC joins the player and travels with them
- **First structure** — player builds or claims something that feels like theirs
- **First NPC death** — someone the player knew (or relied on) is gone
- **First major consequence** — a player action echoes back as a world event (raid retaliation, faction shift, ecological impact)
- **Named relationship** — an NPC reaches high enough favor to feel like a real character, not a function
- **Legendary item acquisition** — a unique item obtained through sustained effort or relationship
- **Monument** — a dead NPC is commemorated in the world; the world acknowledges loss

---

## Difficulty Curve

Difficulty in StarCell is not a tuned slider — it is a property of the world state. The world gets harder or easier based on what the player and the simulation have done.

- Early world: dangerous because the player is weak and relationships are thin
- Mid world: dangerous because the player has things worth losing
- Late world: dangerous because power vacuums and faction conflicts escalate; the player's zone is a target

There is no formal difficulty setting. The player self-selects challenge by where they go and what they provoke. A player who stays in a safe zone and builds will face slow, manageable pressure. A player who hunts deep caves and raids goblin camps will face proportional retaliation.

---

## Win / Loss Conditions

**There is no win condition.** The game continues until the player stops playing. Milestones, legendary items, and society achievements are the equivalent of wins — moments of satisfaction the player defines.

**Loss is not game over.** Player death is a setback: items dropped, progress interrupted, relationships tested. The world keeps running while the player is respawning. NPCs may die defending the player's zone. Structures may be raided. The world does not pause.

**Meaningful loss** — the kind that matters — comes from NPC death, destroyed structures, and ecological collapse. These cannot be undone. That permanence is the point.
